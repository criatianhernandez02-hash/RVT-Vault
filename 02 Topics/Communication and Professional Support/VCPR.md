# VCPR

> [!note] Seeded by Claude before you took Lesson 5
> This is a head start written from the AVMA's and FDA's public definitions, not from your course. After the lesson, compare, fix anything your course says differently, add your own words, and move `status` to `growing`.

> [!abstract] In one sentence
> A VCPR exists when a veterinarian has taken responsibility for an animal's care, knows the animal well enough to make medical judgments, and is available for follow-up; without one, a veterinarian legally cannot diagnose, prescribe or treat.

## What it is
The **veterinarian-client-patient relationship** is the formal relationship between the veterinarian, the owner (the *client*) and the animal (the *patient*). The AVMA's definition has three core parts, and most state practice acts follow it closely:

1. **Responsibility.** The veterinarian has assumed responsibility for making medical judgments about the animal's health, and the client has agreed to follow the veterinarian's instructions.
2. **Knowledge.** The veterinarian knows the animal well enough to make at least a general or preliminary diagnosis, which means they have recently examined it, or have made medically appropriate and timely visits to where it is kept (farms, herds).
3. **Availability.** The veterinarian is readily available for follow-up care if the treatment fails or causes problems, or has arranged for emergency or continuing care.

Two things ride along with those: the veterinarian provides **oversight** of treatment, compliance and outcome, and keeps **patient records**.

## Why it matters for a vet tech
- **Prescriptions.** No VCPR, no prescription or refill: that includes the heartworm preventive a client wants to pick up for a dog nobody has examined in two years. Many states set a time limit (often about a year) after which the veterinarian must re-examine the animal.
- **Extralabel drug use.** Federal law (AMDUCA) only allows a veterinarian to use a drug off-label in an animal inside a valid VCPR; the FDA's own definition of the VCPR is in 21 CFR 530.3(i).
- **Telemedicine.** Whether a VCPR can be *established* remotely (video, phone) is decided by each state and has been changing. Once a VCPR exists, follow-up by phone or video is generally fine. Know your state's rule.
- **Your scope.** Technicians work *under* a VCPR the veterinarian established; a technician cannot create one. In practice: when a client asks you for a diagnosis, a prescription or a prognosis, the answer routes to the veterinarian.

## Key points
- Three parts: responsibility, knowledge, availability (plus oversight and records).
- Established by the **veterinarian**, never by staff; applies per animal, or per herd or group in food-animal practice.
- Required for diagnosing, prescribing, extralabel drug use and most health certificates.
- Time limits and telemedicine rules vary by state: check your state's veterinary practice act.

## Common mistakes and exam traps
- Thinking a phone call from an owner creates a VCPR. In most states it does not.
- Mixing up the **client** (the owner or caretaker) and the **patient** (the animal).
- Assuming the technician can "refill under the vet's name" when the VCPR has lapsed.

## Memory hook
**"The vet is in charge, knows the animal, and picks up the phone."** In charge = responsibility, knows = knowledge, phone = availability.

## Flashcards
#flashcards/communication

What are the three core elements of a VCPR?::The veterinarian assumes responsibility (and the client agrees to follow instructions), has sufficient knowledge of the animal from a recent exam or timely visits, and is available for follow-up or has arranged emergency care.

Who can establish a VCPR?::Only the veterinarian; technicians and other staff work under it but cannot create it.

Which federal law requires a valid VCPR for extralabel (off-label) drug use?::AMDUCA, with the FDA's VCPR definition in 21 CFR 530.3(i).

# Veterinary Technician Credentials

> [!note] Seeded by Claude before you took Lessons 1 and 2
> Written from AAVSB, AVMA and NAVTA public information, not from your course. Compare after the lessons, fix anything your course or your state says differently, then move `status` to `growing`.

> [!abstract] In one sentence
> A credentialed veterinary technician has graduated from an AVMA-CVTEA-accredited program, passed the VTNE, and holds a state credential whose name (RVT, LVT, CVT) depends only on the state; the credential defines what you may do and under whose supervision.

## The veterinary team

| Role | Training | What they can and cannot do (general pattern; your state's practice act decides) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Veterinarian (DVM or VMD)** | Doctorate plus state license | The only person who may **diagnose, prescribe, perform surgery and give a prognosis** |
| **Veterinary technologist** | Bachelor's degree from an accredited program plus VTNE | Same scope as a technician; the degree is longer |
| **Veterinary technician** | Associate degree from an accredited program, VTNE, state credential | Nursing care, anesthesia, lab work, radiographs, dental cleanings, client education and much more, **under veterinary supervision**; may not diagnose, prescribe, do surgery or give a prognosis |
| **Veterinary assistant** | On-the-job training or a certificate (for example NAVTA's Approved Veterinary Assistant) | Restraint, husbandry, cleaning, set-up, some sample handling; allowed tasks vary a lot by state |
| **Practice manager, receptionist (CSR)** | Varies | Front office, scheduling, records, billing, client communication |

Supervision comes in levels that states define differently, usually some version of **immediate** (veterinarian in the room), **direct** (veterinarian on the premises) and **indirect** (veterinarian reachable but not on site). Which tasks need which level is written in the state practice act.

## RVT, LVT, CVT: same job, different state law
- **RVT** (Registered), **LVT** (Licensed), **CVT** (Certified) and **LVMT** (Licensed Veterinary Medical Technician, Tennessee) are the same credential under different state names. California uses **RVT**.
- The title is protected in many states: calling yourself a veterinary technician without the credential can be illegal.
- Renewal usually requires **continuing education** hours every one to two years (California: 20 hours per two years; details in [[Continuing Education Rules - California vs Arizona]]).
- Moving states means applying to the new state board; the VTNE score transfers through the AAVSB.

## The path
1. Graduate from an **AVMA-CVTEA-accredited** program. Penn Foster's associate degree is one.
2. Pass the **VTNE** (Veterinary Technician National Examination), run by the AAVSB: 170 multiple-choice questions, 150 of them scored, in a sitting of about three hours, offered in testing windows several times a year. This vault's [[Topics MOC]] is organized by its ten domains.
3. Apply to your **state veterinary medical board**. Some states add their own law-and-regulations exam; California does.
4. Keep it current with continuing education.

## Going further: the VTS
A **Veterinary Technician Specialist** has earned a rigorous credential from a **NAVTA-recognized academy** after years of experience, case logs and an exam. Specialties include anesthesia and analgesia, emergency and critical care, dentistry, internal medicine, surgery, clinical practice, behavior, nutrition, zoological medicine and more.

## Common mistakes and exam traps
- "Certified" is not a higher level than "registered" or "licensed"; the words only reflect the state's law.
- A technician can *perform* many procedures but cannot *decide* them: diagnosis, prescription, surgery and prognosis stay with the veterinarian.
- Graduating does not make you a technician; passing the VTNE **and** receiving the state credential does.

#flashcards/communication

What four things may only a veterinarian do, not a credentialed technician?::Diagnose, prescribe, perform surgery, and give a prognosis.

What is the difference between an RVT, an LVT and a CVT?::Nothing in the job; the title (registered, licensed, certified) depends on the state that issues the credential.

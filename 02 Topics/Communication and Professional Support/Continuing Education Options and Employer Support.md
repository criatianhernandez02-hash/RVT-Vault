# Continuing Education Options and Employer Support

> [!note] Research done with Claude for [[IVT L01 - Working in the Veterinary Field]] on 2026-11-09
> Conference dates and prices change every year; the ones below were current when found. The write-up, and especially the "how I would ask" part, should be yours. The facts and a skeleton are here.

> [!abstract] In one sentence
> Big conferences give the most hours, hands-on labs and networking at the highest cost; live online gives full-credit interactive hours cheaply; recorded self-study is the cheapest and the most limited (4-hour cap in California); and most employers will pay for some of it if you ask with a plan.

## Where technicians get CE

### National conferences (hundreds of hours on offer, technician tracks, wet labs, exhibit halls)
- **VMX** (Veterinary Meeting & Expo, Orlando, January) and **WVC** (Western Veterinary Conference, Las Vegas, late winter): the two largest in the country. WVC is the closest giant to both California and Arizona.
- **Fetch dvm360**: several cities a year, including **Fetch Long Beach, December 3–5, 2026**, in California, with 100+ hours.
- **AVMA Convention** (summer, rotating cities), and technician-focused one-day events such as the University of Florida's Veterinary Technician CE Conference (about $100–125).

### Regional and state (closer, cheaper, local networking)
- **Pacific Veterinary Conference (PacVet)**, run by the CVMA: **June 18–21, 2026**, with technician tracks and hands-on labs.
- **CVMA** seminars and webinars, and **CaRVTA** (the California RVT Association) events built for technicians.
- **AzVMA** annual conference and webinars in Arizona; **SWVS** (Southwest Veterinary Symposium, Texas, September) for the wider region.

### Online
- **Live, interactive webinars** (count as interactive in California): CVMA, AzVMA, VETgirl, dvm360, VetFolio (NAVTA's education partner), and industry webinars from drug and food companies, which are often free and RACE-approved.
- **Self-paced courses and recorded webinars** (self-study in California, 4-hour cap): VetFolio on-demand, VSPN (the Veterinary Support Personnel Network, VIN's technician arm, low cost), dvm360 on-demand, AtDove (procedure videos), and journal CE articles in *Today's Veterinary Nurse* and the *NAVTA Journal*.

### In-person, local
- Lunch-and-learns from distributor and manufacturer reps at the clinic: often free, often RACE-approved for an hour.
- Wet labs and workshops at RVT programs and community colleges (dentistry, anesthesia, radiography).
- Local VMA chapter dinner meetings.

**One filter above all:** look for **RACE-approved** (AAVSB) on the course, or a provider your state board lists. If it isn't approved, it may not count toward renewal.

## Pros and cons

| Option | Pros | Cons | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **National conference** | The most hours in one trip; hands-on wet labs; meet specialists and VTS technicians; exhibit hall; energizing | Technician registration roughly $300–700, plus travel, hotel, meals and 2–4 days off; overwhelming choice; once a year | Knocking out most of a cycle at once; exploring a specialty; networking |
| **Regional or state conference** | Closer and cheaper; local network and job market; sessions on your own state's law and regulations | Fewer tracks and labs; still travel and time off | Most of your interactive hours at lower cost |
| **Live online (interactive)** | Full credit in California; cheap or free; no travel; recordings often included | Needs a fixed time slot; no hands-on; screen fatigue; easy to half-attend | Filling interactive hours between conferences |
| **Recorded or self-paced online** | Cheapest; any time, any device; rewind | Capped at 4 hours per cycle in California; no questions or hands-on; easy to forget | Topping up; reviewing before an exam or a new role |
| **Local in-person (lunch-and-learns, wet labs)** | Hands-on; free or cheap; the whole team learns together; builds local contacts | Topics follow the sponsor's products; small hour counts; limited schedule | Skills you can only learn with your hands; team CE |

## How employers support CE
What surveys and large employers show:
- **Registration and travel, at least partly.** NAVTA's 2022 demographic survey found over 60% of employers cover some CE registration and travel. A 2019 CVMA survey found a large majority of California RVTs get CE registration fees paid. An Ohio VMA technician survey reported 81% with paid CE.
- **A yearly allowance** is the usual form, typically in the **$500–$1,000** range, sometimes decided case by case. Published examples: Banfield reimburses $750 per year for full-time technicians ($250 part-time) plus paid CE time; BluePearl pays $500 per year for CVTs and $750 for VTS technicians.
- **Paid CE days** (often one to five a year) so you aren't spending vacation on it.
- **In-house CE**: lunch-and-learns, journal clubs and in-clinic training that count toward hours.
- **Guidance choosing CE**: the practice manager or medical director steering you toward what the clinic needs (dentistry, anesthesia monitoring, lab skills), VTS mentoring, and sometimes paid association dues (NAVTA, CaRVTA).

## How to ask an employer for CE support
**When:** at hiring (it's negotiable), at your annual review, or six to eight weeks before a specific course so shifts can be covered.

**Bring a one-page plan with:**
1. **The course:** name, provider, dates, format, and its **RACE approval and CE hours**.
2. **Why this one:** the skill it builds, how the clinic benefits (a new service, fewer referrals, safer anesthesia, a protocol you'll update), and how it fits your required hours.
3. **The cost, itemized:** registration, travel, lodging, meals, and what you'd cover yourself, plus a cheaper alternative (the webinar version, the regional conference) in case the first answer is no.
4. **The schedule impact:** which shifts, who could cover them, and whether you're asking for paid CE days or using your own time.
5. **What comes back:** a lunch-and-learn for the team, written notes or a protocol update, your certificate for the clinic's records.
6. **The policy:** read the employee handbook first. If there is a CE allowance, ask how to use it rather than whether it exists.

A short email skeleton (make it yours):
> **Subject:** CE request: <course>, <dates>
> I'd like to attend <course> (<provider>, RACE-approved, <n> CE hours) on <dates>. It covers <skill>, which would help us <clinic benefit>. The total cost is <$>, broken down as <registration / travel / lodging>; I'm happy to cover <part>. I'd need <shifts> off and have checked that <coworker> can cover. Afterwards I'll run a short lunch-and-learn and share my notes. Could we use my CE allowance for this?

## Flashcards
#flashcards/communication

What single thing should you check on any CE course before paying for it?::That it is RACE-approved (AAVSB) or from a provider your state board accepts; otherwise it may not count.

Name three pieces of information to include when asking an employer to pay for CE.::The course with its RACE approval and hours; the itemized cost; the schedule impact and who covers (plus the benefit to the clinic and what you'll share afterwards).

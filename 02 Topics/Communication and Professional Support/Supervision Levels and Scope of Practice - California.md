# Supervision Levels and Scope of Practice - California

> [!note] Seeded by Claude on 2026-11-09 for [[IVT L02 - Contributing to a Veterinary Team]]
> Built from California's regulations (16 CCR 2034, 2036, 2036.5, 2069) as summarized by the Veterinary Medical Board and the CVMA, plus the AVMA model definitions that textbooks use. Compare it with the lesson, then make it yours. Regulations get amended; for a real decision at work, read the current section on vmb.ca.gov.

> [!abstract] In one sentence
> The veterinarian owns diagnosis, prognosis, prescribing and surgery; the credentialed technician does nearly everything else under a defined level of supervision; the assistant does what the veterinarian delegates, under supervision, with a short list of things they may never do.

## Who does what: the team and its legal scope

| Role | Education and credential | Legal scope, in one line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Veterinarian (DVM/VMD)** | Doctorate; state license | The only one who may **diagnose, give a prognosis, prescribe, and perform surgery**. Legally responsible for every task delegated to the team (16 CCR 2035) |
| **Registered Veterinary Technician (RVT)** | AVMA-CVTEA-accredited program; VTNE; California RVT exam; Board registration | Animal health care tasks under **direct or indirect** supervision; a short list of advanced tasks only under direct supervision; **emergency lifesaving aid** when no veterinarian is available (below) |
| **Veterinary assistant** (unregistered) | On-the-job or certificate; no credential required | Tasks the veterinarian delegates, under direct or indirect supervision as the veterinarian decides; may **not** do the RVT-only tasks; may handle controlled substances only with the Board's **Veterinary Assistant Controlled Substance Permit (VACSP)** and direct supervision |
| **Practice manager, client service (reception), kennel** | Varies | No medical scope; administration, scheduling, records, client communication, husbandry |

Memory hook for the four veterinarian-only acts: **"Diagnose, Prognose, Prescribe, Cut."**

## The supervision levels

Textbooks and most states (following the AVMA Model Veterinary Practice Act and the AAVSB model regulations) use **three** levels:

| Level | Where the veterinarian is | Typical examples (textbook / model rules) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Immediate** | In the room, within sight and hearing of the patient and the technician | Assisting in surgery; anesthesia induction in many states |
| **Direct** | On the premises and quickly available, not necessarily in the room | Most treatments, blood draws, radiographs, dental cleanings |
| **Indirect** | Not on the premises, but has given written or oral instructions and is reachable (phone or similar) | Treatments on hospitalized patients per written orders, follow-up care, some farm calls |

**California uses two levels**, defined in 16 CCR 2034:
- **Direct supervision:** the supervisor is *physically present at the location* where the task is performed and is *quickly and easily available*; the animal has been examined by a veterinarian as good practice requires.
- **Indirect supervision:** the supervisor is *not physically present*, but has given written or oral instructions for the task and is *readily available for consultation*.
- The **supervisor** is a licensed veterinarian, or, where a rule specifically says so, an RVT (an RVT can supervise an assistant for some tasks).

So on an exam that asks about "immediate" supervision, answer with the textbook definition; at work in California, the question is only ever "direct or indirect."

## Which RVT tasks need which level (California, 16 CCR 2036)

| Supervision | RVT may… |
| --- | --- |
| **Direct supervision only** (veterinarian on site) | Induce anesthesia · perform dental extractions · suture cutaneous and subcutaneous tissue (closing existing incisions) · create a relief hole in the skin to place an intravascular catheter · (drug compounding is restricted; see below) |
| **Indirect supervision allowed** (veterinarian reachable, orders given) | Administer controlled substances · apply casts and splints · compound drugs from *non-bulk* substances · and the everyday animal health care tasks: treatments, injections, sample collection, radiographs, catheter placement, dental cleaning, patient monitoring, client education |
| **Never, at any level** | Surgery · diagnosis · prognosis · prescribing · anything else that requires a veterinarian's training |

Two notes: the **supervising veterinarian decides** how much supervision any delegated task needs, and is responsible for the outcome (16 CCR 2035). And the list has been changing in the RVT's favor: a 2021 rulemaking moved controlled substances and casts/splints to indirect supervision, and CVMA-sponsored legislation (AB 516) proposed letting RVTs perform any task not expressly prohibited, dental work included. Whether that passed was not confirmed when this note was written; check before quoting it.

## Emergency care when no veterinarian is available (16 CCR 2069)
An **emergency** (Business and Professions Code 4840.5) is a life-threatening condition where immediate treatment is necessary to sustain life. In one, an RVT may, on their own authority:
1. Apply **tourniquets or pressure bandages** to control bleeding.
2. Give **resuscitative oxygen**.
3. **Establish an open airway**, including intubation, but no surgical airway.
4. Perform **external cardiac resuscitation** (CPR).

**Drugs to prevent or control shock, including IV fluids:** only after *direct communication with a veterinarian*; if the RVT truly cannot reach one, under the *written instructions* of the employing veterinarian. And once started, care given without a veterinarian present may only be **continued under a veterinarian's direction**: stabilize, then get the doctor on the line.

The decision rule in a sentence: **airway, breathing, bleeding, CPR now; drugs and fluids after a phone call; everything after that is the veterinarian's call.** This is what "emergency care decision-making" means for a technician: knowing exactly where your authority ends, and acting fast inside it.

## The professional responsibilities that aren't procedures
- **Client communication:** the technician often spends more time with the client than the veterinarian does; the skills are in [[Client Communication - Rapport and Recommendations]]. Remember the limit: explain and reinforce the veterinarian's plan, don't diagnose, don't quote a prognosis, don't recommend a drug the veterinarian hasn't ordered.
- **Administrative duties:** accurate, timely medical records (what was done, by whom, when, patient's response); controlled-substance logs; consent forms; estimates; discharge instructions; inventory. A record that isn't written didn't happen, legally.
- **Judgment:** knowing when a task you've been handed needs more supervision than you have, and saying so. "I need the doctor on site for that" is a professional answer, not a weakness.

## Flashcards
#flashcards/communication

What four things may only a veterinarian do?::Diagnose, give a prognosis, prescribe, and perform surgery. ("Diagnose, Prognose, Prescribe, Cut.")

Textbook definition of direct versus indirect supervision?::Direct: the veterinarian is on the premises and quickly available. Indirect: the veterinarian is not on the premises but has given instructions and is reachable. (Immediate: in the room, within sight and hearing.)

Name three RVT tasks that require direct supervision in California.::Inducing anesthesia, dental extractions, suturing existing incisions (also creating a relief hole for an IV catheter).

Name two tasks a California RVT may do under indirect supervision that an assistant may not do at all.::Administer controlled substances; apply casts and splints (also compound from non-bulk substances).

In a California emergency with no veterinarian available, what may an RVT do without a phone call, and what needs one?::Without: tourniquets/pressure bandages, oxygen, open an airway (incl. intubation), CPR. With direct communication (or written orders if unreachable): drugs and IV fluids for shock.

# Continuing Education Rules - California vs Arizona

> [!note] Research done with Claude for [[IVT L01 - Working in the Veterinary Field]] on 2026-11-09
> The facts come from the two state boards' rules and FAQs plus the state veterinary associations; where sources disagreed, it says so. Rules change, so before relying on a number for a real renewal, check the board page (links at the bottom). The write-up you submit should be in your own words; the facts are here so you can cite them.

> [!abstract] In one sentence
> California asks RVTs for 20 hours every two years and limits how much can be self-study; Arizona asks CVTs for half that, 10 hours every two years, and renews everyone on the same date, December 31 of even-numbered years.

## California (RVT): the Veterinary Medical Board

| | California |
| --- | --- |
| Credential and board | **Registered Veterinary Technician (RVT)**; California Veterinary Medical Board (VMB) |
| Renewal cycle | Every 2 years |
| Hours required | **20 hours** of approved CE per 2-year renewal period |
| First renewal | **Exempt.** No CE is needed for your very first renewal after you register |
| Online | Allowed. **Interactive** online CE (live webinars, courses with real-time instructor interaction) counts in full. **Non-interactive self-study** (recorded videos, journal articles, self-paced online modules, even ones with a quiz at the end) is capped at **4 hours** per cycle, so at least 16 of the 20 hours must be interactive, in person or live online |
| Topic limits | At most **15 hours** per cycle in practice-management or stress-management courses (the regulation text as reported by CVMA and PacVet; confirm with the Board) |
| Other ways to earn hours | Teaching an approved course or writing board-exam questions, with caps; academic coursework converts (1 quarter unit = 10 hours, 1 semester unit = 15 hours) |
| Who can provide it | Providers approved under 16 CCR 2086.1: RACE-approved providers (AAVSB), the AVMA, the CVMA and its chapters, accredited veterinary and RVT programs, and others on the list |
| Paperwork | You certify completion when you renew; keep your certificates, because the Board audits a sample of registrants |

## Arizona (CVT): the Arizona State Veterinary Medical Examining Board

| | Arizona |
| --- | --- |
| Credential and board | **Certified Veterinary Technician (CVT)**; Arizona State Veterinary Medical Examining Board |
| Renewal cycle | Every 2 years, and everyone together: certificates expire **December 31 of even-numbered years** (next: December 31, 2026) |
| Hours required | **10 hours** per 2-year cycle (veterinarians need 20). Several secondary sources agree, and the Board's rule confirms it indirectly by requiring 5 hours for a shortened first cycle |
| First renewal | **Prorated, not exempt.** A technician certified in an even-numbered year completes 5 hours before the first renewal |
| Online | Interactive programs, including interactive online programs, count hour for hour. Whether non-interactive self-study is capped for technicians was not settled by the sources found; check A.A.C. R3-11-401 |
| Topic limits | None found for technicians. (Veterinarians who hold a DEA registration must complete 3 hours on opioids and substance use each cycle; technicians cannot hold DEA registrations, so it does not apply) |
| Who can provide it | CE from the AzVMA, the AAVSB (RACE-approved) and similar recognized providers is accepted for technicians |

## The main differences

| | California RVT | Arizona CVT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours per 2 years | **20** | **10** |
| First renewal | Fully exempt | Prorated (5 hours if certified in an even year) |
| Recorded / self-study online | Capped at 4 hours; the rest must be interactive | No technician cap found (confirm) |
| Topic caps | 15 hours max on practice management or stress | None found |
| Renewal date | Your own 2-year cycle | Everyone: December 31 of even years |
| Title | Registered (RVT) | Certified (CVT) |

The pattern: California regulates *how* you earn the hours (interactive versus self-study, topic caps) as much as how many; Arizona asks for fewer hours with fewer strings but puts everyone on one calendar. For a technician moving between the two, the surprises would be California's self-study cap (a stack of recorded webinars won't do) and Arizona's shared December 31 deadline.

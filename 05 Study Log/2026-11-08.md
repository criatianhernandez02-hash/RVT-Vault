> **Lesson 1, Working in the Veterinary Field** (you read it in the portal; this is from its research tasks and the Credentials note)
> - A credentialed technician = accredited program + VTNE + state credential; the title (RVT in California, LVT, CVT elsewhere) is the state's choice, not a rank.
> - Credentials have to be kept: California asks 20 CE hours every two years with only 4 hours of self-study allowed; Arizona asks 10, everyone renewing December 31 of even years. Employers usually pay for some CE if you ask with a plan.
> - Lifelong learning is part of the job, and conferences, live webinars, recorded courses and local wet labs each trade cost against hands-on value.
>
> **Lesson 2, Contributing to a Veterinary Team**
> - The team: CSC, technician, kennel attendant, practice manager, veterinarian(s). Four things never leave the veterinarian: **diagnose, prognose, prescribe, cut**. Everything else a technician does is under supervision.
> - Three supervision levels: **immediate** (vet in the room: surgery assist, anesthesia induction), **direct** (vet on site: anesthesia monitoring, dentals), **indirect** (vet reachable, working under protocols: routine treatments, radiographs, lab work). **Emergency** is the exception: lifesaving aid under indirect supervision, then call the vet as soon as possible. California law uses only direct and indirect, and puts extractions and anesthesia induction under direct.
> - The technician's job is wider than procedures: client care (history, questions, explaining the vet's plan, education) and administration (records, scheduling, billing, inventory, controlled-substance logs, radiation logs, compliance).
> - "A crucial bridge": 2–4 technicians per vet, ~30 patients a day for the vet versus ~10 for you, so you're the one who tracks each patient and connects vet, client and animal.
> - Beyond general practice: VTS specialties through NAVTA-recognized academies (equine is yours), the AALAS ladder (ALAT → LAT → LATG), and a dozen other settings.
> - Your research: in California an RVT may extract teeth under direct supervision; the AVMA, AAHA, AVDC and AVDT all set a stricter standard, and the AVDT bans it for its members outright.
>
> **Three ideas to keep from tonight:** the four vet-only acts; the three supervision levels plus the emergency rule; and that being heard comes before being right when you talk to clients and to your veterinarian.

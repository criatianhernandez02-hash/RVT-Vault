# Equine VTS Pathway

> [!note] Researched with Claude on 2026-11-09 from the academy's own FAQ and secondary write-ups
> The hour requirements have changed over the years and the sources disagree on details, so the numbers below are "about this much"; the exact current rules are on aevnt.org. Re-check them the year you plan to apply.

> [!abstract] In one sentence
> The credential is **VTS (Equine Veterinary Nursing)**, granted by the **Academy of Equine Veterinary Nursing Technicians (AEVNT)**, a NAVTA-recognized academy; it takes a credentialed technician roughly four years of mostly-equine work, a case log, case reports, equine CE, a skills checklist and letters, then a comprehensive exam.

## What the academy asks for
| Requirement | What it means | What the sources say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Credential** | You must already be a credentialed technician (RVT in California) and hold it for the whole qualifying period, right up to the application | All sources agree |
| **Experience** | Years of credentialed work with the large majority in **equine nursing** | The AEVNT FAQ describes about **8,000 hours** of technician work (roughly four full-time years) with the majority in equine nursing; older sources quote 3 years/5,000 hours or 4 years/8,000 hours with 5,000 equine. Use the academy's current number |
| **Skills checklist** | A list of equine nursing skills you must have performed, signed off by a supervising veterinarian; gaps can be filled with supplemental time at another facility | AEVNT FAQ |
| **Case log** | A log of equine cases you managed over about a year | About 50 cases |
| **Case reports** | Detailed written reports on selected cases from the log | Four to five, depending on the source |
| **Continuing education** | RACE-approved CE in equine topics in the years before applying | About **50 hours** over the preceding 3–4 years |
| **Application packet** | Letter of intent, resume, proof of credential, skills checklist, CE record, two letters of recommendation, fee | Historically due around **May 1**; verify |
| **Exam** | A comprehensive written exam after the packet is accepted | Given once a year; an incomplete application must be fixed and resubmitted the following year |

The review committee is made up of current VTS (EVN) holders. Expect the process to take about a year from packet to exam.

## Where equine technicians work
Ambulatory equine practices (truck-based, farm calls), equine referral hospitals and university teaching hospitals (anesthesia, ICU, surgery, neonatal foal units), racetracks and breeding farms, sport-horse practices, and mixed large-animal practices. California has serious equine medicine: the UC Davis Large Animal Clinic and the racetrack and breeding regions. Case logs are easiest to build in a hospital setting; ambulatory work builds the handling and field skills.

## The professional home along the way: AAEVT
The **American Association of Equine Veterinary Technicians and Assistants (AAEVT)** is the equine technicians' association (the AEVNT grew out of it). It offers student membership, equine-specific CE, a meeting alongside the AAEP (equine veterinarians') convention, and an online equine certification program that is a reasonable stepping stone years before the VTS.

## A realistic roadmap from here
| When | Step |
| --- | --- |
| **Now (Semester 1)** | Pay extra attention to large-animal content in A&P 1 and 2 (the lessons on the digestive and musculoskeletal systems matter most in horses). Join AAEVT as a student member. Get hands on horses: a barn, an equine rescue, a riding school; handling confidence is the first thing equine practices look for |
| **Semesters 2–4** | Aim an externship at an equine or mixed practice. Start a personal equine skills list (restraint, IV catheter, bandaging, lameness exam assisting, nasogastric tubing, anesthesia monitoring). Take equine CE when it's cheap or free (AAEVT webinars) |
| **Graduation → RVT** | Pass the VTNE and the California exam; register. The clock for the academy starts only once you're credentialed |
| **Years 1–2 as an RVT** | Work where the horses are: an equine hospital if possible (case volume), or mixed practice with a heavy equine load. Keep a running case log from day one, even informally; it is painful to reconstruct later |
| **Years 2–4** | Make the log formal, collect the 50 CE hours in equine topics, get the skills checklist signed as you go, pick the cases for your reports while they're fresh |
| **Year 4–5** | Submit the packet (spring), sit the exam (that year), VTS (EVN) |

At the plan's pace, graduation lands in late 2028 or 2029; the earliest realistic VTS (EVN) is around **2033**. That sounds far until you notice that every year between now and then has a concrete job on this list.
